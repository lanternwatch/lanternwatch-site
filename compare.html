Lantern Watch — How We Compare

Most parental controls are either too complex, too expensive, or built for a cloud company's bottom line. Here's how Lantern Watch stacks up against the tools families actually encounter.

Lantern Watch

Open-source, runs directly on your router. Per-device scheduling, social media blocking, push notifications, and a friendly dashboard — all free, forever. No subscription. Your browsing data never leaves your home. No cost.

Free — always

Pi-hole

The gold-standard DNS ad blocker. Powerful for technical users but requires a separate device (Raspberry Pi), no scheduling, no parent-friendly dashboard, and no notifications out of the box.

Free (hardware extra)

AdGuard Home

Excellent DNS filtering — and it's the engine underneath Lantern Watch. On its own, though, there's no per-device scheduling, no push alerts, and setup is technical. Lantern Watch adds all of that on top.

Free

Bark

Best-in-class AI social media monitoring via its cloud service — scans 30+ platforms for cyberbullying, predators, and self-harm language. Requires subscriptions, cloud accounts, and on-device agents. Excellent for monitoring; lighter on hard blocking.

$5–$14/month + hardware

Circle Home+

A parental control device that connects to your router using ARP-spoofing. Friendly UI with time limits and filtering, but requires a paid subscription after the first year and has known network stability issues.

$129 device + $99/year

NextDNS

Cloud-based DNS service with strong filtering lists and per-device profiles. No hardware to buy, but every DNS query leaves your home and goes through NextDNS's servers. Paid tiers needed for unlimited use.

Free tier; $19.90/year paid

OpenDNS Family Shield

Free DNS-level content filtering — set it and forget it. No scheduling, no per-device controls, and all traffic routes through Cisco's servers. Great as a backup layer but not a full parental solution.

Free
